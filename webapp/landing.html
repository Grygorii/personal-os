The problem

Two weeks later, it's gone.

You keep the cover, the mood, maybe one good line. The ideas quietly leave — and you never notice, because feeling like you've read a book is indistinguishable from having kept it.

Highlighting doesn't stop it. Nor do note-taking apps. They record the book; they don't put it in you.

Being asked is what makes it stick. Psychologists call it the testing effect: retrieving an idea fixes it in memory far more firmly than reading it again ever will.
Kept is that test — for the books you actually read.

The rule

It will not flatter you.

An assistant that tells you what you want to hear is worse than none at all. Bluff an answer and Kept scores it under 40 — then tells you exactly which chapter to go back to.
The first score is usually humbling. That is rather the point.
Questions
Is it free?

Free to start, with a daily allowance that grows as you use it. Later you can support it from €3 a month — or connect your own free AI key and keep it free for good.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Sign in with Google and it runs in your browser. If you'd rather have an icon, add it to your home screen and it opens like any other app.

What does it actually ask me?

Five questions written for the book you just read — two on its core ideas, two on applying them to your own life, and one where you argue with the author. Nothing you could answer from the blurb.

Who can see my books and notes?

Only you. Nothing leaves your library unless you share a result, and even then only the book, the score and a thought you pick. You can export or delete everything at any time.

Does it work with any book?

Any book, in any language. The exam is written for the specific book you read — fiction, history, business, anything.
