The real reason

It was never about time.

There's a book on your table you haven't opened in a month. Not because you're busy — you found the hours for worse things. It's quieter than that: you already know it won't stay.

Nine hours, and by Christmas you'll have the cover and nothing else. Put like that, reading stops feeling like building and starts feeling like pouring water into sand.

You keep the cover, the mood, one good line — and never notice the rest go. Feeling like you've read a book is indistinguishable from having kept it. Highlighting doesn't help; notes don't either. They record the book, they don't put it in you.

Being asked is what makes it stick. Psychologists call it the testing effect: retrieving an idea fixes it in memory far more firmly than reading it again ever will.
Kept is that test — for the books you actually read.
The check

It will not flatter you.

When you turn the last page it asks five questions written for that book — two on its ideas, two on using them in your own life, and one where you argue with the author. Bluff an answer and it scores you under 40, then names the chapter to go back to.

Questions
Is it free?

Free to start, with a daily allowance that grows as you use it. Later you can support it from €3 a month — or connect your own free AI key and keep it free for good.

Do I read the books in Kept?

No — there are no books in Kept, and it doesn't sell or lend them. You read however you already do: paper, Kindle, a library copy, an audiobook. Kept is where you jot what struck you while you're reading, and where you get tested once you've finished. It never asks you to change how you read.

Do I need to install anything?

Not unless you want to. Sign in with Google and it runs in your browser straight away. Adding it to your home screen gives it an icon and opens it like any other app — which is where you'll actually use it, since that's where you read.

Why isn't it in the App Store?

Because it doesn't need to be. Tap Add to your phone and Kept installs from this page in one step — an icon on your home screen, opening full screen, no download and no updates to sit through. It also means when you tell me something's wrong I can fix it that day, instead of waiting a week for a review. If enough people ask for a store listing, I'll make one.

What does it actually ask me?

Five questions written for the book you just read — two on its core ideas, two on applying them to your own life, and one where you argue with the author. Nothing you could answer from the blurb.

Who can see my books and notes?

Only you. Nothing leaves your library unless you share a result, and even then only the book, the score and a thought you pick. You can export or delete everything at any time.

Does it work with any book?

Any book, in any language. The exam is written for the specific book you read — fiction, history, business, anything.
