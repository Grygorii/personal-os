# Word bank

The gap you named: you add a word with **one** meaning and miss the others. Here,
every sense goes in. Confusable pairs sit side by side so the difference is obvious.

<a id="to-too-two"></a>
## to / too / two

- **to** — (1) direction/goal: *I'm going **to** work.* (2) the marker before a verb
  (the infinitive): *I want **to** learn.* (3) up to a point: *soaked **to** the skin.*
- **too** — (1) also: *I'm coming **too**.* (2) more than is wanted (excess):
  *This is **too** hard.* Memory hook: **too** has an extra *o* = *too much*.
- **two** — the number 2. *I have **two** tickets.*

Quick check: *"It's ___ late ___ call him."* → **too** late **to** call.

<a id="son-sun"></a>
## son / sun  (homophones — sound identical)

- **son** — a male child. *Her **son** is at school.*
- **sun** — the star the Earth orbits; sunlight. *The **sun** is out.*

Yours slipped into a message to your manager. They sound the same; only meaning
tells them apart. If you mean a person → **son**. If you mean the sky → **sun**.

<a id="knew-new"></a>
## knew / new  (homophones)

- **knew** — past tense of **know**. *I **knew** the answer.*
- **new** — not old; recently made. *a **new** laptop.*

If it's about knowledge in the past → **knew**. If it's about something recent → **new**.

<a id="weather-whether"></a>
## weather / whether  (homophones)

- **weather** — rain, sun, temperature. *The **weather** is cold.* (Also a verb:
  *to **weather** a storm* = get through hardship.)
- **whether** — introduces a choice between options, like "if": *I don't know
  **whether** to stay or go.*

<a id="checkout-check-out"></a>
## checkout / check out

- **checkout** (one word) — noun or adjective: *the **checkout** page*, *at **checkout***.
- **check out** (two words) — the verb: *Please **check out** the new update.*
  Also: leave a hotel (*we **check out** at 11*), or borrow (*check out a library book*).

Rule of thumb: if you could put a word between the two parts (*check it out*), it's
the two-word verb.

<a id="immerse"></a>
## immerse

- **immerse (yourself) in** — to involve yourself completely: *I **immerse myself
  in** English.* Needs a reflexive pronoun (myself/yourself/…) when you do it to
  yourself. Passive state form: *I **am immersed in** English.*
- literal sense — to dip something fully in liquid: *immerse the pan in water.*

## Word web — words explained with words you know

The rule you discovered: a definition is useless if it uses another word you don't
know ("nuance" explained with "subtle"). So each entry is written in plain words, and
lists **what it leans on** — the simpler words underneath it. We dig down every branch
until we hit solid ground you already own. That's the puzzle completing.

Every word you **tap** in the study deck lands here, with all its senses. Nothing is
lost or scattered — it all connects.

<a id="nuance"></a>
### nuance *(noun)* — say "NEW-ahnss"
A very small difference in meaning or feeling that is easy to miss.
*"His feedback had a nuance the summary missed."*
Leans on → **subtle**.

<a id="subtle"></a>
### subtle *(adjective)* — say "SUT-ul" (silent *b*)
So small or quiet that it is hard to **notice**. Opposite of *obvious*.
*"a subtle change in his voice"* = a change so small you'd miss it if you weren't looking.
Leans on → **notice** (to see / become aware of something) → solid ground.

**The chain:** nuance → subtle → notice → known. Climb back up and you own all three.
