The mistakes that repeat. Each one is a tracked item in `progress.json` — I test
these cold until they stop happening. This is a mirror, not a scolding.

<a id="comma-splice"></a>
## Comma splice — your #1 punctuation leak

**What it is:** joining two complete sentences with only a comma.

- ❌ `I finished the report, I sent it to my manager.`
- Both halves are full sentences. A comma alone can't hold them together.

**Four correct fixes** (pick by meaning):

1. **Period** — two separate thoughts: `I finished the report. I sent it to my manager.`
2. **Semicolon** — closely linked thoughts: `I finished the report; I sent it to my manager.`
3. **Comma + coordinating conjunction** (FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so):
   `I finished the report, so I sent it to my manager.`
4. **Subordinator** — make one half depend on the other:
   `After I finished the report, I sent it to my manager.`

**Test yourself:** if the words on *each* side of the comma could stand alone as a
sentence, a bare comma is wrong. Full lesson: [`rules/02-comma-splices.md`](rules/02-comma-splices.md).

<a id="tense-shift"></a>
## Tense shift mid-sentence

**What it is:** starting in one tense and sliding into another for no reason,
usually when typing fast.

- ❌ `Yesterday I open the ticket and escalated it.` (present → past)
- ✅ `Yesterday I opened the ticket and escalated it.` (both past)

**Rule:** within one connected action, keep the tense unless the *time* actually
changes. Deliberate changes are fine and necessary:
- ✅ `I finished it yesterday, so now I feel relieved.` (past → present, because the
  time genuinely moved from yesterday to now.)

**Test yourself:** read the verbs alone — do they sit in the same time? If not, is
there a real reason time moved?

<a id="spelling-precision"></a>
## Spelling precision at typing speed

Not laziness — a set of specific words your fingers get wrong. We drill the
**words**, not "spelling" in the abstract. Current high-frequency offenders live
in [`words.md`](words.md#spelling-slips). The pattern is retired only when the list
comes back clean cold.

**Method:** I dictate or prompt the word in a sentence; you type it; we fix and log
any that miss. Each stubborn one can graduate to its own tracked item.
