# Articles — a / an / the

Articles flag whether a noun is **general** (any one) or **specific** (that one).
Three choices: **a/an**, **the**, or **nothing** (the "zero article").

## a / an — one, non-specific

Use for a *single, countable* noun the listener can't yet pin down — introducing it
for the first time. **a** before a consonant *sound*, **an** before a vowel *sound*
(it's the sound, not the letter): *a university* (sounds like "yoo"), *an hour*
(silent h), *a one-off* (sounds like "wun").

- *I need **a** ticket.* (any ticket) · *She's **an** engineer.*

## the — specific, both of us know which

Use when the noun is already identified — mentioned before, unique, or clear from context.

- *I need **the** ticket.* (the specific one we're discussing)
- *The sun is out.* (only one) · *Close the door.* (the obvious one)

## zero article — general/uncountable/plural-in-general

No article with uncountable nouns and plurals used *in general*, and with most proper names.

- *I like ___ coffee.* (coffee in general) · *___ Reports are due Friday.* (reports in general)
- *___ Accenture*, *___ Ireland* (proper names take no article)

## Institutions — the rule you've drilled (5/6 ✓)

Some place-nouns (**university, hospital, school, church, prison, bed**) drop the
article when you mean them **for their normal purpose**, and take **the** when you
mean the specific building/visit.

| Normal purpose (no article)        | The specific place (the)                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| *She's **at university**.* (studying) | *I parked at **the** university.* (the site) |
| *He's **in hospital**.* (a patient)   | *I met her at **the** hospital.* (the building) |
| *They go **to church**.* (to worship) | ***The** church* was locked. (the building)  |

Note: this "no article" habit is British/Irish English (your environment).
American English says *in **the** hospital*. For Cambridge, the British pattern is safe.

Proper-noun institutions still take no article: *University College Dublin*, *Accenture*.
