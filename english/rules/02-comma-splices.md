# Comma splices & joining sentences

Your most frequent punctuation error, in both casual and work writing. Worth owning
completely, because it also teaches clauses — which C2 grammar is built on.

## The building block: independent clause

An **independent clause** is a group of words that could stand alone as a sentence:
it has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought.

- *I finished the report.* ✅ complete
- *the report.* ❌ no verb · *Because I finished the report* ❌ "because" leaves it hanging

## The error

A **comma splice** joins two independent clauses with *only a comma*:

- ❌ *I finished the report, I sent it to my manager.*

A comma is too weak to hold two complete sentences together. You need a stronger join.

## The four legal joins

| Fix                              | Example                                                      | Use when…                       |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **1. Period**                    | *I finished the report. I sent it to my manager.*           | two separate thoughts           |
| **2. Semicolon**                 | *I finished the report; I sent it to my manager.*           | tightly linked, equal thoughts  |
| **3. Comma + FANBOYS**           | *I finished the report, **so** I sent it to my manager.*    | you want to show the relationship |
| **4. Subordinator**              | ***After** I finished the report, I sent it to my manager.* | one idea depends on the other   |

**FANBOYS** = the 7 coordinating conjunctions: **f**or, **a**nd, **n**or, **b**ut,
**o**r, **y**et, **s**o. A comma goes *before* them when they join two full clauses.

**Subordinators** = after, before, because, although, while, when, since, if, etc.
They turn one clause into a dependent one, so a comma is enough.

## The trap: conjunctive adverbs

Words like **however, therefore, moreover, then, also** *feel* like they can join
sentences with a comma. They can't — they're adverbs, not conjunctions.

- ❌ *It was late, however I finished it.*
- ✅ *It was late; however, I finished it.*  (semicolon before, comma after)
- ✅ *It was late. However, I finished it.*

## The self-test

Cover the comma. Can the words on **each side** stand alone as a sentence?
- **Both** stand alone → a bare comma is **wrong**; use one of the four joins.
- One side can't stand alone (starts with *after/because/while…*) → the comma is fine.

## Test yourself (cold)

Fix each — say which of the four joins you used and why:
1. *The client called, they were not happy.*
2. *We fixed the bug, however the report still failed.*
3. *I checked the logs, everything looked normal.*
4. *She's fluent in speech, she rarely studies grammar.*
