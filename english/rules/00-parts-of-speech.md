# Parts of speech — the foundation

Every word in English does one of a small number of *jobs*. Grammar rules are just
statements about how these jobs fit together. If the label is fuzzy, every later
rule stays fuzzy — so we nail this first. There are **8** core categories.

| Part            | Its job                                   | Examples                       |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Noun**        | names a thing, person, place, idea        | team, Dublin, report, courage  |
| **Verb**        | an action or a state of being             | send, escalate, be, seem       |
| **Adjective**   | describes a noun                          | urgent, overdue, calm          |
| **Adverb**      | describes a verb, adjective, or adverb    | quickly, very, remotely        |
| **Pronoun**     | stands in for a noun                      | I, you, it, they, myself       |
| **Preposition** | shows relation (place, time, direction)   | in, on, to, after, of          |
| **Conjunction** | joins words or clauses                    | and, but, so, because, although|
| **Article**     | flags a noun as specific or general       | a, an, the                     |

## The key idea: a word's part depends on its *job in the sentence*

The same word can change category. This is what trips people up.

- *I **report** to my manager.* → **verb** (an action)
- *I sent the **report**.* → **noun** (a thing)
- *I **check out** the update.* → **verb** (two words)
- *the **checkout** page* → **adjective/noun** (one word)

So the question is never "what part is this word?" — it's "**what is this word doing
here?**" (See the checkout/check out split in [`words.md`](../words.md#checkout-check-out).)

## Adjective vs. adverb — the one to watch

- **Adjective** describes a *noun*: *a **quick** reply.*
- **Adverb** describes a *verb* (often ends in -ly): *she replied **quickly**.*

❌ *She replied quick.* → ✅ *She replied quickly.* (describing *how she replied* = a verb → adverb)

## Why this matters for C2

Later rules are stated in these words: "a comma splice joins two independent
*clauses*"; "put an *adverb* here, not an *adjective*"; "this *preposition* follows
that *verb*." Once the labels are automatic, the rules read like plain instructions.

## Test yourself (cold)

For each **bold** word, name its part of speech and say what job it does:
1. *The **overdue** invoice worried the **team**.*
2. *He **quietly** escalated it.*
3. *I'll deal with it **after** lunch.*
4. *She's fluent, **but** she's never studied grammar.*
